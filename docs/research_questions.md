# Research Questions

## Primary research question

How can statistical methods improve the reliability and interpretability of
business ratings compared with simple average-star rankings?

Here, "improve" must be demonstrated against explicit criteria rather than
assumed. Candidate criteria include out-of-time predictive performance,
calibration, ranking stability, and clearer communication of uncertainty.

## Subquestions

1. How do observed rating distributions and review counts vary across businesses,
   categories, locations, and time periods represented in the data?
2. How much do arithmetic-mean rankings change when low-volume businesses are
   adjusted toward a transparent reference mean?
3. How does statistical uncertainty vary with review count, rating dispersion,
   and plausible dependence or selection effects in reviews?
4. How stable are business rankings under new-review arrival, resampling, and
   reasonable choices of reference group and prior strength?
5. Do adjusted ratings outperform unadjusted averages in time-aware historical
   evaluation, and which baselines and scoring rules support that conclusion?
6. Which presentation choices help decision-makers distinguish score level,
   evidence volume, uncertainty, and ranking stability without implying false
   precision?

## Scope guardrails

The project will not infer customer intent from invented evidence, claim causal
effects from observational ratings alone, or publish real findings before the
Yelp Open Dataset has been obtained, validated, and analyzed under its terms.
